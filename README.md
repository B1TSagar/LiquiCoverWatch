# Cover Display for Galaxy Watch

An iPod-style Cover Flow for whatever your phone is playing. Turn the bezel to flip through covers, press Back to open one, and triple-press Back to jump to Now Playing.

The project has two halves of one app, Cover Display:

| Module | What it is |
|---|---|
| `wear/` | The Galaxy Watch app (Wear OS, Kotlin + Compose). |
| `mobile/` | A small phone companion. It reads what's playing in any music app and feeds it to the watch. |
| `shared/` | The data models and Data Layer protocol both halves use. |

The watch faces are a separate project, **LiquiWatch**, with its own companion app. Cover Display only hands those faces the playing song's blurred cover, its colour and where the song is, as complications, and tells LiquiWatch's companion the song's name, artist and cover (round) for the Expedition face's date pill (see *Watch faces* below).

## Controls

| Screen | Bezel | Back ×1 | Back ×2 | Back ×3 | Touch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cover Flow** | Moves through covers, one per click. Clockwise moves covers right to left. | Flips the highlighted cover over to show its album | Same as ×1 | Now Playing | Tap the center cover to open it, tap a side cover to move, or swipe sideways. Swipe down for recommended songs, up for liked songs. Two-finger tap switches between the player's covers and the recently played. |
| **Recommended / Liked** | Moves through covers | Flips the highlighted cover over to show its album (pick the song there) | Back to the covers you came from | Now Playing | As Cover Flow; swipe back the way you came (up from Recommended, down from Liked) to return |
| **Album (flipped cover)** | Moves the highlight through the tracks | Back to Cover Flow | Plays the highlighted song and opens Now Playing | Now Playing | Tap a row, drag up/down |
| **Now Playing** | Phone volume. Counter-clockwise is louder, so the volume bar along the right edge fills the way you turn. | Play / pause | Back to the previous screen | Scrub mode | Previous, play/pause and next buttons. Tap the title for lyrics, tap the cover to rate. |
| **Now Playing, scrub mode** | Moves a target time around the ring. The song keeps playing normally. | Cancel, back to Now Playing | Jump the song to the target time | Cancel | — |
| **Lyrics** | Scrolls by hand. Lyrics follow the song again 4 s after you stop. | Back | Rate the song | Now Playing | Drag to scroll |
| **Rate** | Picks 0–5 stars. Nothing is saved yet. | Back, without saving | Saves the rating, shows "Saved ✓", then goes back | Now Playing | Tap a star, then **Save** |

Back ×3 always opens Now Playing, from any page (on Now Playing itself it opens scrub mode). When the screen has dimmed, a single Back press only brightens it, but that press still counts towards a double or triple press, so ×3 works straight away.

**Two-finger tap:** put two fingers on the screen together, then lift them without sliding. Whatever the first finger was about to do, such as tapping a cover, is cancelled.
- **On Cover Flow** it switches between the player's own covers (its queue, or the songs up to the playing one) and the recently played songs. The covers dissolve into a blur and the other set sharpens out of it, the centre pane growing back in like a droplet. Each set opens on the playing song or the newest recent one. Your choice holds while you open albums and come back, until the music starts, stops or has been paused for a while. With nothing playing there's only the recently played, so the watch just gives a short buzz.
- **On any other page** it opens Lyrics. Back ×4 does the same, on every page. On the Lyrics page, Back ×2 opens Rate for the playing song.

**Recently played after a long pause:** once the song has been paused for 2½ minutes, Cover Flow shows the recently played songs instead of the player's covers, as it does when nothing is playing. If Cover Flow is open at that moment it switches over; otherwise it opens on them. The phone says when the song was paused, so this works even if the watch app was closed meanwhile. A two-finger tap brings the player's covers back. Change the wait with `RECENT_AFTER_PAUSE_MS` in `Tuning.kt`.

**Recommended and liked songs:** on Cover Flow (the player's covers or the recently played), swipe down with one finger for songs recommended by the app that played last, or up for songs liked in it. As you pull, the row of covers follows your finger and "RECOMMENDED ↓" or "↑ LIKED" comes in at the edge; a tick says you've pulled far enough, and letting go there opens it (letting go sooner springs back). They're a Cover Flow of their own, labelled RECOMMENDED · APP or LIKED · APP, best or newest first. Back ×1 flips the highlighted cover to its album, where you pick the song to play in that app; Back ×2, or swiping back the way you came, returns to the covers you were on. Each time you open them the phone looks them up afresh (the watch shows the last list at once meanwhile), and a line at the bottom says where they came from for a few seconds. Where the songs come from:
- **The app's own library first:** the one Android Auto browses, with its recommendations and its liked songs, when the app lets other apps in. Many big apps only let Google's and the car makers' apps in, though.
- **Recommended, otherwise:** YouTube Music's radio for the last songs played in the app (what YouTube Music would play after them), merged. For YouTube Music that's its own, and each opens straight on its own video when picked. For any other app, such as Apple Music, it's a guide to songs like the ones you played there: only songs proper (not music videos), played in that app as any picked song is. If the radio has too few, songs like the artists played lately from Deezer's free artist radio are used, and failing that, more songs by those artists from Apple's catalog. Songs you played lately are left out. (Lists an app offers that turn out to be playlists or stations rather than songs aren't used.)
- **Liked, otherwise:** the apps don't share their liked songs, so the phone collects them as you go: every song it sees liked in its app (the heart, thumb or like button in its controls) while Cover Flow runs, and every song you give 3 stars or more on the watch (1 or 2 stars take it off), newest first, up to 300 per app.

**Home ×2:** opens Lyrics, once you set up the shortcut (see below).

- **Covers:** each cover keeps its mirror reflection underneath, and the domed crystal still stretches the covers near the screen's edge. One pane of liquid glass, a little larger than a cover, sits at the centre, and the covers move into and out of it. Its sides are liquid: the side a cover leaves by stretches out after it, bowing outward, and the side a cover comes in by is pushed in, bowing inward; each springs back straight, with a little bounce, once the cover is out or in. Meanwhile the pane bends the light harder. The highlighted cover always shows its song, artist and album, and the playing song is marked **▶ NOW PLAYING**. `COVER_CASE_DP` in `Tuning.kt` sets how far the pane reaches past the cover.
- **Background:** every screen sits on a cover, blurred and darkened as on Now Playing: the playing song's, or in Cover Flow the highlighted album's (it follows the highlighted cover once the bezel settles and fades from one cover to the next; the cover turns over to reveal it on the track list). When nothing is playing, the messages on Now Playing, Lyrics and Rate show the song being started, or else the last one played. Only Cover Flow's "connecting" and "phone app needed" messages (no songs known yet, so no cover) stay black.
- **Liquid glass:** the controls are real refracting glass, modelled on Apple's Liquid Glass and Figma's Liquid Glass kit, drawn by one GPU shader per screen (Wear OS 4 / Android 13 and later). Each piece of glass has a thick rounded rim that gathers in what lies around it and wraps it along the inside of its outline, so the track titles and the covers bend into contours that follow the glass, with a short rainbow along the bottom edge. Light catches the rim: a soft glow inside the edge, a hair-thin highlight (brightest top left and bottom right) and a faint darker outline, and the curved rim is lighter where it faces the light and darker where it turns away. Buttons, labels and cards are frosted, milky glass; track rows are clear. On the album's track list each track is two pieces of clear glass, a bubble for its number and a capsule for its name. The selection is a pair of droplets filled with the album colour (a gradient), a touch bigger than the pieces they sit on: like the Rate page's star droplet, they thin out and bend the light harder as they leave one track, then grow back onto the next. While the list is still every piece stands apart; while it rolls, the pieces melt into one another through smooth liquid necks and pinch apart again as it settles. On Now Playing the progress is a slider built like the volume bar, after Apple's Liquid Glass sliders: a thin ring of glass round the edge with a coloured line centred inside it (a set share of the ring's thickness, as the volume bar's level fills its bar) from the top up to a clear oval glass knob, a little thicker than the ring, that magnifies and bends the line under it. The knob glides along with the song (worked out as often as it moves half a pixel, so it never steps) and, when the position jumps (a seek, a scrub step, a new song), springs there like the Rate page's droplet, stretching along its way and bending the light harder until it settles. Both sliders take their colour from the album: a lighter tone of the cover's colour (a slightly darker one for very pale covers, silver for black-and-white ones), blending into the next album's when the song changes. Turning the bezel shows the volume along the right edge as a curved bar of frosted glass, filled with the album colour from the bottom up to a clear glass knob at the level. The bar runs just inside the progress ring and down to the Next button, keeping the same hair's-breadth gap from both (the Next button's gap is measured from where the button really sits); its rim still catches the progress line's light. The knob has its own rim inside the bar, magnifies the end of the fill, and flows to each new level the same way. The transport buttons are separate drops of glass (frosted, with play/pause in the album colour, like the sliders). They follow the curve of the progress ring, each sitting the same hair's-breadth gap from it as the volume bar (the play button a little lower than the others; the row moves to keep the gap whatever the screen and text sizes), and the progress knob melts into a button's drop (or the clock's pill) through a liquid neck as it passes over it; using one, by tapping it or with Back for play/pause, makes it swell and melt into the buttons beside it through thick necks whose sides curve smoothly from one button into the next, while clear liquid glass runs out and spreads over them until it covers almost all of each, with the button's colour flowing along inside and filling most of each, all melting into the buttons' own glass as one body of liquid (no lens edges inside them) (`SPILL_NECK_COVER` and `SPILL_COLOR_COVER` in `NowPlayingScreen.kt`), then drain back into separate drops. The clock sits on a pill of smoked glass at the top of Cover Flow, Now Playing, Lyrics and Rate; on Now Playing the same pill shows the volume while you turn the bezel, or SCRUB in scrub mode, and its width flows to fit. The lyrics page has no other glass: just the words over the blurred cover, with the clock on its glass pill. Buttons and cards grow in like droplets, and pages dissolve into each other. All moving glass follows the same spring as the Rate page's droplet (quick, smooth, with the slightest liquid settle): the volume lens, the progress knob, the button spill, and glass growing in (the lyrics scroll on it too). `GLASS_SPRING_DAMPING` and `GLASS_SPRING_STIFFNESS` in `Tuning.kt` set it. The whole face sits under a slightly domed crystal. Older watches get a simpler see-through look. In `Tuning.kt`: `GLASS_BEND` (how much the rims bend), `GLASS_LIGHT` (light on the rims), `GLASS_SHADOW`, `GLASS_MERGE_DP` and `TRACK_MERGE_DP` (how far pieces reach to fuse) and `GLASS_SCREEN` (the domed face); 0 turns a part off. Besides rounded shapes (whose sides can bow in or out, as Cover Flow's pane does), the glass can be a curved tube with round ends (the sliders' knobs, the progress ring and the volume bar) or a chain of beads, and a piece can be a lens that keeps its own outline inside other glass (the sliders' knobs).
- **Haptics:** every cover, or track row, that passes the center gives a haptic tick, whether you moved it with the bezel, a tap or a swipe.
- **Speed follows the bezel:** covers chase the bezel with a spring that never jumps or overshoots. Turn it slowly and covers glide one at a time. Turn it faster and the covers change exactly as fast as the song title does: the centre cover is always the one the title names, and the covers stop the moment the bezel stops. In `Tuning.kt`, `FLOW_RESPONSIVENESS` sets the slow glide, and `FLOW_MAX_LAG` and `FLOW_MAX_RESPONSIVENESS` set how tightly fast spins keep up.
- **Covers are prepared on the phone:** the phone does all the work on a cover before sending it: it cuts the picture to exactly the size the watch draws it at (a little larger than the centre cover, so it's only ever scaled down) and packs it as WebP, and picks its colour. Each cover then crosses Bluetooth as one small message, a fraction of a full-size JPEG, and the watch only has to show it. When the list loads, the watch asks for all the covers it's missing in one request, and the phone answers nearest first, preparing a few at once.
- **Sharp covers:** the phone keeps covers at up to 400 px in full colour. Music apps often attach only small thumbnails to the songs in their list. Those show first so the list fills in quickly, and are then swapped for a sharper copy of the same picture as the phone finds one (see *Finding covers* below).
- **Covers are cached:** when the list loads, every cover in it is loaded into memory, nearest first, so scrolling never shows a placeholder. Covers are also kept in the watch's storage as the phone prepared them, up to 800 by default, so they appear instantly next time without asking the phone. (After this update the watch fetches its covers once more in the new form.)
- **Scrub mode:** the glass knob springs along the edge to show where you'll jump to, while a small white dot keeps moving where the song really is. Nothing changes in the song until you double-press Back.
- **Lyrics:** the current line is sharp and bright. The line just before it and the line just after it are sharp but dimmer, and the rest are blurred and dimmer still, so lines ease in and out of focus as the song moves on. The view scrolls with the song. `LYRICS_SHARP_NEIGHBORS` and `LYRICS_NEIGHBOR_ALPHA` in `Tuning.kt` set how many lines stay sharp and how bright they are. The lyrics sit over the blurred cover with no glass on top, and the current line stays in the middle of the screen as the song plays. Each line lights up right as it starts (`LYRICS_LEAD_MS` early), timed to the moment rather than checked every so often. The watch measures how far its clock is from the phone's each time the app opens (song positions are stamped with the phone's clock, and the two can be apart), and the phone resends the position whenever the player's drifts by more than 0.3 s.
- **Rate:** clockwise adds a star and counter-clockwise removes one. Each star sits in its own circle of clear glass. A droplet of gold glass marks the rating: it springs from star to star, thinning and bending the light harder as it leaves one star and melting briefly into the circles it passes, then grows back to full size on the new star, which turns gold as it arrives. Saving changes the song's like in the app it's playing from:
  - **3 stars or more** likes it.
  - **1 or 2 stars** puts it back to not liked: a full favourite star or heart becomes empty. In apps with a thumbs-down (YouTube Music), it's a thumbs-down.
  - **No stars** leaves likes alone.

## Home button shortcut for lyrics

Wear OS never passes the Home (top) button to apps, so an app can't react to Home presses itself. Galaxy Watches do let you choose what a Home double-press opens, and the watch app installs a second entry called **Lyrics** for this. Set it up once:

1. On the watch, go to *Settings → Advanced features → Customize keys → Double press*.
2. Choose *Open app* and pick **Lyrics**. Menu names vary a little between One UI Watch versions.

After that, Home ×2 opens the lyrics of whatever is playing, from anywhere on the watch. There's no Home triple-press option on Galaxy Watches. Inside the app, a two-finger tap opens the lyrics with no setup. To reach Rate, press Back ×2 on the Lyrics page, or tap the cover on Now Playing.


## Watch faces

The watch faces live in their own project, **LiquiWatch**, and Cover Display doesn't need them. What Cover Display gives watch faces, any face and not just LiquiWatch's:

- **Album art:** the watch app hears the phone's player even with Cover Display closed. The phone does the work: it blurs and darkens the cover (a little less blurred than behind the app's own screens, so the cover reads behind the face), picks its colour, and sends both ready to show (a small JPEG and one colour, made again if a sharper copy of the cover turns up). It sends them to any watch whose face has shown the album art without waiting to be asked: the next song in the queue's a few seconds into the song before (the watch keeps it aside, ready), so the face switches the moment that song starts, and any other song's as it starts. The watch only keeps them and hands them on: it never blurs or compresses a picture itself. It keeps the last eight songs' (each a few KB, as the phone sent it, the last three decoded and ready), so a song played again goes up on the face from what the watch has, at once, and the phone, which keeps count of what each watch has been sent lately, doesn't send it again (should the watch not have it after all, it asks, as above). If a song's background hasn't come 0.7 seconds after the song, the watch asks the phone; until it comes, the face stays as it is. The watch hands the picture to faces as a full-screen photo complication (or a small image in photo style, for a face that asks for that). A pause takes the album art away a second and a half later (it used to wait 5 seconds, then a quarter of a second, which some players' skips outlasted: they report "paused" for up to 1.3 s between songs); a skip doesn't, as the phone reports a song being skipped to, or loading, as between songs rather than paused. Any face with a photo slot can use **Album art** as its background. These complications are only ever sent as the music changes, never on a schedule: the system saves up scheduled updates while the screen is off and asks for them all as it wakes, which stalled the face for a moment after waking.
- **Kept awake for the face:** a Galaxy Watch freezes an app it isn't showing a few seconds after the app has gone idle, and a frozen app can't be handed the phone's messages. In a log of the watch, the watch app was frozen (`freezePackage com.coverflow.app ... reason: UidIdle`) and Google Play services then couldn't hand it the phone's messages as songs started (`sent binder code 18 with flags 2 to frozen apps and got error -74`), so the face never heard a song had started: no cover, no colour, and no song on LiquiWatch's date pill. While a LiquiWatch face shows the compass, the LiquiWatch companion (which runs in the foreground then, for the live compass) keeps the watch app bound (`FaceKeeperService`, which does nothing else), and an app bound by one running in the foreground isn't idle, so the watch doesn't freeze it. As it's bound, the watch app catches up: it takes the phone's last state (the Data Layer keeps it on the watch) if it's newer than the last it heard, and while a song plays asks the faces for the album art again and tells the companion what's playing. Whatever else wakes the watch app (the phone's message, the companion asking what's playing, Cover Display being updated) holds it until what it asked of the faces has gone: 0.4 s at the most for each of the phone's messages, as the next one waits meanwhile (a song's cover usually comes a moment after the song). A face counts as showing the album art for a week after it last asked for it, as well as while the watch says one of its slots holds it: when a face was put on again or updated, the watch could tell the old one's slots had gone after it had told the new one's had come, and they're the same slots, so nothing was asked of the faces any more and the album art never came back. The companion is told what's playing whether a face shows the album art or not, and when it asks, or Cover Display is updated, the faces are asked for everything again.
- **How quickly a new cover shows:** the phone sends a new song to the watch 50 ms after its player's burst of changes settles (it was 250 ms, then 120, then 80). Watches whose face shows the album art hear of it first, in a small message of their own sent the moment the player changes (a new song, play, pause, a skip or a seek), ahead of the synced state, which is slower to arrive; whichever comes second is left alone if it's older. The phone starts making the song's face background as soon as the song is picked (while it loads, not once it plays), makes the next song in the queue's ahead of time and sends it to the watch a few seconds into the song before (the watch keeps it aside, decoded, and puts it up the moment that song starts, so the phone doesn't send it again then), and sends a new cover's face background before Cover Flow's own copy of the cover, which is several times its size. On the watch, the part of the app that hears the phone never waits on the phone any more: if a new song's background hasn't come by itself 0.7 seconds after the song, the phone is asked for it in the background (it used to ask at once and wait there while the request went out, so the background the phone had already sent waited behind it). On Wear OS 6 the watch hands the cover, its colour and the progress straight to the watch's complication system (complications library 1.3.0, through the Wear SDK). Before, each went as a broadcast to the Wear OS app, which passed it on to the system's complication service, which then bound to Cover Display to ask for it: about 150 ms of the watch's work each on a Galaxy Watch4, several times that while it was short of memory (measured), and a new song is three of them. On an older watch it still goes the old way. Each goes just after one of the LiquiWatch · Expedition face's seconds hand's beats (eight a second, a third of the way through each eighth): everything on that face changes as the seconds hand beats, so an update between beats would have the watch draw a frame of its own for it. Each waits for the moment that, by how long they've lately taken to arrive, has the face get it a little after the next beat, and those asked for meanwhile go with it. But each beat takes only so much (the cover 3, its colour 3, the progress line 1, in a budget that fills up by 3 a beat, holding 4 at most, while the watch keeps up with ease; by 2 or 1, holding 3, while it's busy), and what doesn't fit rolls on to the next beat: so a new song's cover and progress line go in one beat and its colour in the next (or a beat or two later while the watch is busy), not all in one frame. How busy the watch is, the app measures all the time: how late its beat-timed asks get to go, and how much longer than usual they take to arrive. The cover goes first, then its colour, then the progress; nothing waits more than a second. As the music starts (from a pause, or from nothing), the colour, the heaviest change for the face (it draws its hands, compass and glass again in it), waits for the watch to settle: the watch's own Now bar slides in over the face just then, and that's when the watch is busiest. The watch app checks its main thread every 40 ms, and once that has run on time (no more than 8 ms late) three times running, after 0.45 s at the least and 1.4 s at the most, the colour goes; the cover and the progress line go at once, as before. Skipping from song to song while the music plays doesn't wait (the Now bar's up already). LiquiWatch's companion hears, with the song, until when this work will keep the face busy, and sends the date pill's name and cover just after; and until when the watch may still be settling, and holds back its own work meanwhile. When the cover hasn't reached the watch yet, the album art complication hands the face the last cover at once and asks the phone in the background (it used to wait for the phone's answer first).
- **The phone does the pictures:** Cover Display's watch app never blurs, resizes or compresses a picture. The phone's app makes the face's background (the cover blurred and darkened, 120 px square, the size the blur is worked out at: a soft blur, so the face scales it to the screen without losing anything; it used to be scaled up to 256 px on the phone first, which came out on the face the same, give or take the faint noise in it, from a picture four and a half times as big for the watch to keep, hand to the face and send to its graphics chip with every song: 57 KB now, not 256 KB, and its JPEG about 3 KB) and its colour, and the cover round for the face's date pill (38 pixels, the size the face shows it, so the watch never scales it: the cover's middle square shrunk in halves, each averaging its pixels, then the last of the way, and its circle's edge smoothed, as a PNG with clear corners, about 2 to 3 KB, after the background in the same message, where a watch app that doesn't know of it stops reading), and the watch only keeps them, decoded once as they arrive, and hands them to the face. (The watch used to make the background itself whenever a song started before the phone's had come, so the face could switch at once: a blur, a JPEG and a decode on the watch at the very moment the face was drawing the change. The next song's background now comes ahead instead; any other song's comes with it, and until it does the face stays as it is.) What Cover Display does on the watch as a song changes runs at the usual priority: it ran at background priority for a while, to leave the watch's two cores to the face as it drew the change, but a thread at background priority all but stops while the face keeps both cores busy, as it does with music playing and the compass turning, and the album art then waited until Cover Flow was opened. A complication never answers a face with nothing at all (it sends "no data" instead): the library waits for an answer that nothing never gives, and the update, with any asked for alongside it, would never reach the face. The background is kept decoded, so the album art complication answers as quickly as the colour and the progress and the three reach the face together, rather than the colour first and the picture a moment later. A sharper copy of the cover the face shows, in the same colour, sends only the picture again: the colour too used to go again, and with it the face drew its hands, compass and glass again for nothing. The same background sent twice (once ahead, once with the song) is noticed and left alone. As a song changes the face is sent only what changed, as each update is work for the face while it draws the new cover: the cover; the colour only if it's another (the next song from the same album keeps it, and a new colour has the face draw its hands, compass and glass again); and the progress once the new song plays (skipping to a song leaves the line as it was until then, so it isn't sent at the skip and again as the song starts). Cover Display notes what it does for the face in the watch's log (tags CoverFlowFace and FaceUpdates).
- **Album colour:** the playing cover's colour (worked out on the phone with the background), toned exactly as Now Playing's progress and volume sliders are (dark colours lighter, very pale ones a little darker, greys a light silver), as a full ranged value in that colour. Watch faces can't be handed a colour as such, so it comes two ways: as the colour ramp, and as a number, the minimum, which is the colour's red, green and blue as one number (red × 65536 + green × 256 + blue, exact in a float). While nothing plays the minimum is below zero, with no colour. LiquiWatch · Expedition reads the number and paints its hands' lines, its compass glass and its knob with it.
- **Song progress:** where the playing song is, in that same album colour, as a ranged-value complication: its minimum and maximum are when the song started and when it ends (seconds since midnight), and its value is where the song was when it was sent. It's sent only when the music plays, pauses, skips or seeks, never in between: every update makes the watch work out part of the face again, and on the Watch4 Classic one a second added to the face's stutter. LiquiWatch · Expedition doesn't need more: it draws the line round its compass glass with a clear glass knob, like Now Playing's progress slider, working out where the song is from its own clock, eight times a second. (On other faces the ring stays where the song was at the last play, pause, skip or seek.) While nothing plays (a second and a half after a pause; a skip doesn't count) it's still a ranged value, but one that starts below zero (−1 to 0, at −1) with no colour: that's how a face can tell nothing plays and go back to its own colours, which it couldn't do with no data at all.
- **What's playing, for LiquiWatch's date pill:** the LiquiWatch · Expedition face shows the playing song's name and artist on its date pill, with its cover, round, in the pill's window, and a tap there opens Cover Display straight on Now Playing. A face can't read one complication's text from another's slot, so Cover Display's watch app tells the LiquiWatch companion on the same watch what's playing, with the round cover (a broadcast to that app alone, with Cover Display's identity on Wear OS 5 and later, which the companion checks), and the companion's own complications carry it. It's told only when that changes: as a song starts or the music skips to another, when a song's cover comes after the song, and once a pause has lasted as long as the album art takes to go (a skip doesn't count). The song's name and artist come from the phone with the small message of the change itself (they used to come only with the synced state, a moment later, and meanwhile the pill went back to the date for a moment). The companion asks again when it starts afresh, and it's told again after Cover Display is updated. The pill's tap opens Now Playing through an entry of its own (`NowPlayingLauncher`, an alias of the app's activity that isn't in the app list), as the Lyrics entry opens the lyrics.
- **No taps:** the album art, album colour and song progress complications have no tap action. On LiquiWatch · Expedition the album art and the song's slots lie under the whole face, so a tap on the face's free space used to open Cover Display; now it does nothing. Open Cover Display from the watch's app list, or, while a song plays, from LiquiWatch's date pill (that tap is the LiquiWatch companion's). (After Cover Display is updated, the faces are asked for these complications again at once, so a change like this shows without waiting for the music to change.)

## Setup

You need Android Studio Koala (2024.1.2) or newer, an Android phone, and a Galaxy Watch4 or newer paired to that phone.

1. **Open the project.** In Android Studio, choose *File → Open* and select the `CoverDisplay` folder, then let Gradle sync. If Studio offers to upgrade the Android Gradle Plugin, it's fine to accept.
2. **Install the phone app.** Turn on USB debugging on the phone and connect it. Pick the `mobile` run configuration and press Run.
3. **Grant access on the phone.** Open *Cover Display* on the phone and tap **Grant access**, then switch Cover Display on. If the switch is greyed out, go to *Settings → Apps → Cover Display → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings*, then try again. Then tap **Link watch**, allow **Nearby devices**, and confirm your watch in Android's dialog, so songs you pick on the watch can start even when the music app isn't running. (Nearby devices lets the phone app find the watch among the phone's paired devices and hand the dialog its address: the dialog's own search never lists a watch that's already paired and connected, and matching it by name fails once it's been renamed in the phone's Bluetooth settings. Linked to something else by mistake, the card says what, and **Link a different device** starts again.) Then, under **Keep running in the background**, tap **Allow** and say yes to Android's prompt (Cover Display's battery use becomes "Unrestricted"; a linked watch has Android do that by itself too). Without it, a Samsung phone freezes Cover Display in the background and later closes it, so the watch hears of no new song, and its face's album art doesn't change, until Cover Display is opened on the watch. Uninstalling and reinstalling Cover Display (rather than installing over it) takes both the link and this away: allow it again then.
4. **Put the watch in developer mode.** On the watch, go to *Settings → About watch → Software information* and tap *Software version* about 5 times. Then:
   1. Go to *Settings → Developer options* and turn on **ADB debugging** and **Wireless debugging**.
   2. Tap *Pair new device*. The watch shows a pairing code and an IP:port.
5. **Connect the watch to Android Studio.** The watch and the computer must be on the same Wi-Fi.
   - In Android Studio, use *Device Manager → Pair devices using Wi-Fi*.
   - Or from a terminal, run `adb pair <ip>:<pairing-port>` and enter the code, then run `adb connect <ip>:<port>` using the port shown on the Wireless debugging screen.
6. **Install the watch app.** Pick the `wear` run configuration, choose the watch as the device, and press Run.
   - **Use the release build for everyday use.** Debug builds of Compose apps run several times slower, and on a watch that shows as lag. Open *View → Tool Windows → Build Variants* and set both `wear` and `mobile` to **release**, then press Run as before. The release build is optimized and shrunk by R8 and signed with the same debug key, so it installs from Android Studio just the same and the phone and watch apps still match. It also installs Compose's ready-made startup profiles and one for all of the app's own code (`wear/src/main/baseline-prof.txt`: compiled ahead rather than as it runs, which on the watch's two slow cores would compete with the face as a song changes), so the app gets smoother still once the watch has compiled them (overnight while charging, or straight away with `adb shell cmd package compile -m speed-profile -f com.coverflow.app`).
7. **Try it.** Play something on the phone, then open **Cover Display** on the watch.

> **Important:** install both halves from the same computer. The Wear Data Layer only connects apps that have the same `applicationId` (`com.coverflow.app`, set in both `build.gradle.kts` files, and kept from when the app was called Cover Flow, so an installed copy updates in place) and the same signing key. Debug and release builds from one machine share a key automatically (the release build is signed with the debug key). To sign with your own keystore, sign both apps with it.

## How it works

1. **Reading the phone.** The phone app uses notification access, which is the only way Android lets an app read other apps' media sessions. It does not read your notifications. From the active player it gets the song, artist, album, artwork, playback state and, if the player shares it, the queue.
2. **Sending to the watch.** That state goes to the watch as a Data Layer item, so the watch has something to show the moment it opens. Covers are sent on demand as JPEGs of up to 400 px, and the watch caches them.
3. **Finding covers.** The player's own artwork is used first. If the player doesn't share artwork for queued songs, the phone looks the cover up on Apple's iTunes Search API. You can turn this off on the phone screen.
   Every image is checked before it's used, because players don't always attach the right one:
   - **Stale art:** right after a song change, art identical to the previous song's is ignored until the real cover arrives.
   - **Placeholders:** if the same picture shows up on songs from two different albums (an app logo or a grey note), it's blacklisted. (Albums, not artists: a soundtrack's songs credit different singers but share one cover.) Every cover that used it is deleted on the phone and the watch, and the catalog cover replaces it.
   - **Flat images:** single-colour images are treated as placeholders, so a real cover wins.
   - **Thumbnails:** many players attach only a small thumbnail (often 60–200 px) to the songs in their list. The phone sends it at once, then looks for a sharper copy in the background. It tries the player's own link to the image first, asking the image server for a bigger size (this works for YouTube Music, YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music and Deezer links), then the iTunes catalog. A copy is only used if it's recognisably the same picture as the thumbnail, in both colours and layout, so a catalog mismatch can't swap in the wrong cover. The watch also asks again about small covers it saved earlier.
   - **Updates:** a better cover that arrives later replaces the old one on the watch.
4. **Lyrics.** The phone fetches synced lyrics from [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net), a free, open lyrics database, and caches them. Songs it doesn't have show plain lyrics, or a "no lyrics found" note. The same online switch on the phone controls this.
5. **Ratings and likes.** Star ratings are stored on the phone per song, so they work with any player. When the player supports it, they're also sent to the player:
   - **Likes:** saving 3 stars or more likes the song in the player, and 1 or 2 stars removes the like (or gives a thumbs-down where the player has one). This goes through the player's rating API or its own buttons (a heart, thumbs up/down, love or favourite). Buttons that toggle are only pressed when their label shows the song is in the other state, or when Cover Flow pressed it itself, so nothing flips by accident.
   - **Stars** are only sent if the player takes 5-star ratings, which few streaming apps do.
   - The Rate page says whether a 3-star-plus rating will also like the song in the player.
6. **Album tracklists.** The back of a flipped cover lists the album's tracks from the iTunes catalog, merged with what's in your queue.
   - Tracks already in the queue play directly.
   - Other tracks are requested with the player's *play from search*, the same mechanism voice assistants use. Most big players support it, but results depend on the player.

## When nothing is playing

If nothing is playing on the phone when you open Cover Display:
- **The covers:** you get your recently played songs, starting on the most recent one. Each is labelled **RECENT · <app>**, the app it was played in.
- **Playing one:** pick a song the usual way (open the cover, then Back ×2 on the track). It plays in the app it came from, and Now Playing shows "Starting in <app>…" until it begins.
- **If music stops while you're on Now Playing, Lyrics or Rate:** the watch goes back to these covers, with the song that was just playing selected.
- **What counts as nothing playing:** no app playing, and no paused app still open. Many music apps keep their last song loaded after you swipe them away, so a paused app only counts as open while its music controls are still in the notification shade. Close the app or swipe its notification away and, within about 10 seconds, the watch switches to the recently played songs. (This needs Cover Flow's notification access to be connected. Android sometimes doesn't reconnect it after the app is updated; the phone then asks it to, says so on its screen, and meanwhile counts a paused app as open only for 3 minutes after it last played.) A paused app whose notification is still showing keeps its own covers, so you can resume it, for 2½ minutes; after that Cover Flow shows the recently played songs, and a two-finger tap brings its covers back.

To start a picked song, the phone tries these in order, and after each one it checks for a moment that the app really is playing that song before moving on. Big music apps such as Spotify and YouTube Music take play and pause from other apps but usually ignore "play this song" requests from apps they don't know, and woken up they just resume what they played last. Some only show the song when asked to play it. What works best is opening the app with the song's own link, so the phone remembers each app's ID for every song it sees playing or queued:
1. **Ask the app:** if the app still has its media controls (playing, paused, or left behind after it was closed), it's asked for what every app takes: skip to the song in its queue, or play it if it's already loaded. Then it's asked to search for the song and play it (what YouTube Music, Apple Music and most apps take from other apps; a search gets several seconds, since the app looks it up online), and to play it by the app's own ID or link for it; whichever worked last time for that app goes first. Every way is tried before moving on. If the search makes it play its own closest match (another take of the song), that's kept rather than searching all over again.
2. **Open the app with the song:** with its exact link when it's known (a YouTube Music watch link, a Spotify track link), otherwise with a "play this song" request, the way voice assistants do. The phone then waits for the app to start the song. If nothing plays after a few seconds (the app may just be showing the song), the now-open app is asked through its media controls as in step 1. Android only lets a background app open another app if it's linked to a companion device or may display over other apps. On the phone screen, **Link watch** links your watch through Android's own dialog. That's the recommended way; **Or allow display over other apps** works too (on Android 15 the phone shows an invisible 1-pixel overlay for a second while opening the app, which that permission now requires).
**YouTube Music** takes only its queue through its media controls (song requests are left to Google's own apps) and shares no IDs for its songs, so it's handled differently: if it's running, the song is skipped to in its queue (or played if already loaded); otherwise it's opened with the song's YouTube Music link. It often opens on the song without starting it (it may have no media controls until it plays), so if nothing plays within 5 seconds the phone presses play, every 5 seconds, a different way each time: on its media controls, its own Play button (as headphones do), then the system's Play key (only when no other app has music controls). Only the picked song counts, and only once it has really played for a moment (a song YouTube Music can't play shows as loading, then it says it's unavailable and moves on to another). If pressing play brings back the song it played before, it's paused and the phone keeps waiting. If YouTube Music settles on another song by itself (the link was unavailable, or to another song), it's paused (better silence than the wrong song), that video is never used for the song again, and the next match opens (up to 3 links per pick, about 50 seconds in all). The links are found by searching YouTube for the song, in your phone's own country (taken from the mobile network, then the SIM, so a video blocked where you are isn't picked): with your own free YouTube Data API key if you add one on the phone (the official, dependable way; the card on the phone explains the three steps), otherwise through YouTube Music's own web search (unofficial, so it could stop working if YouTube changes it). Results are ranked by title (video titles like "Full Video: Song | Film | …" are cut down to the song's name), artist or channel, and length; a result by someone else only counts with the very same title and length (so a cover, or another song merely named like it, isn't picked). Up to 3 matches are kept per song, best first, and each is first checked with YouTube Music's own player (all at once): that it can be played in your country, and that its title, channel and length are the song's. A song that plays everywhere is checked alongside: if even that one doesn't pass (YouTube changed something, or wants a sign-in), the check isn't working from here, and no match is turned down as unplayable. If YouTube Music itself shared a song's ID while it played or queued it, that exact video goes first of all. If the search finds nothing playable, the song is looked up on the iTunes catalog (or Deezer's) and its YouTube Music link asked of [Songlink](https://odesli.co) (song.link), a free service that matches songs across music apps, and checked the same way. Songs are looked up ahead of time, one every few seconds in the background (each song while it plays, and the recently played ones), and links found are remembered, so picking a recently played song starts it at once (links found by earlier versions are looked up again once, with the stricter checks). A song that can't be found isn't looked up again in the background for 6 hours (picking it tries again). Lookups follow the same online switch as covers and lyrics. Only if no link is found does it fall back to the steps above and below.

3. **Wake the app quietly**, only when it can't be opened: press the system's Play key (like a headphone button; only when that app was the last one playing), connect to its media service like Android Auto does, or press its Play button, then ask as in step 1. If, a few seconds later, it's still only playing the song it played last, it's paused again, since silence is better than the wrong song.

The phone remembers what worked for each app and tries that first next time; an app's own ID for a song it has played before goes first of all (it needs no online search, so it's the quickest), unless that app passed over IDs last time. The steps listed on the phone screen show how many seconds into the pick each one came. If the app ends up playing something, that's what the watch shows. If nothing plays, the watch says why and what would help, but only after the phone has watched 15 seconds more (25 with the phone locked) in case the app is still starting the song: an app opened behind the lock screen, or slow to load the song, can start it well after being asked. If the song starts even after that, the message on the watch goes away. An app opened while the phone is locked (or its screen is off) stays on top behind the lock screen, and while it's on top, YouTube Music and others don't put up their media notification, so the lock screen and the watch get no controls. So once the song plays, the phone brings its home screen up behind the lock screen: the app carries on in the background and puts its notification up (if it pauses on the way, play is pressed again), and unlocking shows the home screen rather than the app. After YouTube Music is opened with a song's link, what it settles on counts as the song if it's named the same once video words are cut away, or if it's the same length to within a second and a half (another upload of the song, or its title in another script). The phone screen lists what happened on the last try, step by step, under **Play songs when nothing is playing** (for YouTube Music, including which links it had and what each lookup found), and **Copy these steps** puts them on the clipboard to paste into a message. Songs played before this update have no app recorded, so they play in the app you used most recently, and songs from before Cover Flow started remembering the app's IDs can only be searched for until you've played or queued them once. Picking from a song list while an app is playing skips straight to that song in its queue.

## Always-on display

While Cover Display is open and music is playing, the screen never goes dark. After 15 s without input it dims to a fifth of its brightness, keeping the same screen as it was, with everything still moving (the covers, the progress, the lyrics):
- **Waking up:** any touch, bezel turn or button press brightens the screen again. That first press only wakes the screen and doesn't also do anything.

Cover Display does this itself, so it works even if the watch's own Always On Display doesn't keep apps on screen. After music has been paused for 2 minutes, the screen is allowed to time out normally again.

The trade-off is battery: the screen stays on, at a fifth of its brightness, showing the full screen.

`Tuning.kt` has the settings:
- `KEEP_SCREEN_ON` turns the feature off.
- `DIM_AFTER_MS` sets the idle time before dimming.
- `DIM_FRACTION` sets the dim level, as a share of the brightness before dimming.
- `KEEP_ON_AFTER_PAUSE_MS` sets how long the screen stays on after pausing.

If the screen does time out, for example after a long pause, the watch's own Always On Display can still show the same screen, at a fifth of its brightness. That needs *Always On Display* turned on in the watch's Display settings.

## Liking songs in your music app

Saving 3★ or more on the watch likes the song in the app that's playing it. 1–2★ puts it back to not liked: a full favourite star or heart becomes empty again, and in apps with a thumbs-down it's a thumbs-down. "Not interested" buttons are never pressed, since they shape the app's recommendations rather than rate the song. Apps expose likes in different ways, and Cover Flow tries them in this order:
1. **Rating API:** the standard Android rating API, if the app supports it.
2. **Media controls:** a like, love or favourite button among the app's media controls, or a thumbs-down.
3. **Notification:** the same kind of button on the app's media notification.

**If likes don't reach an app** (for example Apple Music):
1. Play a song in that app.
2. Open the phone app and find the **Liking songs in …** card.
3. Tap **Try** next to each button while watching the music app.
4. Select the button that favourites the song. Tapping **Try** again undoes it.

The phone app has a **Liking songs in …** card that shows every button the playing app offers. If Cover Display picked the wrong one, or none, tap the right button there and Cover Flow uses it from then on. Some buttons don't show whether the song is already liked. For those, Cover Flow presses the button only once per song, so it can't un-like by accident. If an app offers no buttons at all, the card says so, and likes stay in Cover Flow.

## Things to know

- **Album track lists:** every time an album's track list opens (and each time you come back to it), the watch shows the copy it kept from last time at once and asks the phone afresh. The phone looks the album up on the catalog again: of the editions that could be it (named exactly as the player names it first, then the fullest), the first that actually has the song in it, so a single, an EP or a compilation with a similar name isn't taken for the album. A store only lists the tracks it has, which can be fewer than the album has in Apple Music, so when your country's store lists fewer tracks than the album has, the US store's list is used if it's fuller. The phone then adds any of the album's songs the player itself has (from its queue when it played the album) that the catalog edition lacks, and, from the copy it kept before, only songs of that very album. It keeps the fullest list each album has had, and so does the watch, so an album opened from the recently played songs, when the player's queue can't help and the catalog may not be reachable, still shows all its tracks. A partial list (only the songs the phone knew) never replaces a full one, and is asked for again while the album is open.

- **Smoothness:** moving things are redrawn, not rebuilt. As the covers turn or the track list rolls, the covers and rows are only moved and redrawn on each frame (which ones exist changes only as a whole cover or row passes), the lyrics are rebuilt only when the line changes, and the scrub-mode clock ticks on its own. The glass, its backdrop and scrolling text each sit in a layer of their own, so a scrolling title redraws just itself and the glass is worked out again only when it or what's behind it changes (or when a glass element really moves).

- **Back has a short delay.** One to three presses act about 0.4 s after your last press, because the app waits to see if another press is coming. A fourth press acts immediately. You can change the wait with `MULTI_PRESS_WINDOW_MS` in `wear/.../Tuning.kt`.
- **Two-finger tap timing:** the second finger must land within 0.25 s of the first, and both must lift within 0.6 s. Change these with `TWO_FINGER_TAP_MAX_GAP_MS` and `TWO_FINGER_TAP_MAX_MS` in `Tuning.kt`.
- **Both side buttons can't be used.** Galaxy Watches take a screenshot when both buttons are pressed together. Holding Back opens Samsung Wallet, and touching both buttons starts a body-composition reading. The watch handles all three itself, and apps can't change them.
- **Swipe-to-dismiss is off,** so swipes don't fight the Back-button navigation. Leave the app with the Home key.
- **If Back does nothing,** your watch's Back key may be set to open recent apps. Change it under *Settings → Advanced features → Customize keys*. The menu names vary a little between One UI Watch versions.
- **Ignoring apps.** If YouTube, a podcast app or a browser keeps taking over the watch, switch it off under **Music apps** in the phone app. Its playback is then ignored, it can't be controlled from the watch, and the watch shows the next app that's playing. Apps appear in the list once they've played something; installed music apps are listed from the start. **Clear recently played** empties the recently played list.
- **The song list depends on your player.** If the player shares its queue, you get the queue: earlier songs to the left, upcoming songs to the right. If it doesn't, you get the songs you've played since installing the companion, with the current one on the far right. The phone app shows which mode you're in.
- **Haptics too weak or too strong?** Change `TICK_MS` and `TICK_STRENGTH` in `Tuning.kt`. If you feel nothing, check vibration intensity in the watch's *Sounds and vibration* settings.
- **Tuning.** All of these are in `wear/src/main/java/com/coverflow/wear/Tuning.kt`:
  - Cover Flow: cover size, side-cover angle, spacing, reflection height, glass pane margin (`COVER_CASE_DP`).
  - Background: brightness and fade speed.
  - Bezel: direction, detents per step, volume direction (`VOLUME_FOLLOWS_ARC`).
  - Scrub: step size.
  - Haptics.
